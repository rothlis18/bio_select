The tension between evolved female mate-selection strategies and contemporary egalitarian cultural norms is rooted in the mismatch between ancestral preferences for high-status, dominant partners and modern social conditioning that discourages traditional dominance-submission hierarchies. Parental investment theory and protection-resource trade-offs have shaped human mating psychology to favor partners who can provide resources and protection, leading to evolved preferences for high-status, dominant individuals. Hypergamy, the tendency for women to seek higher-status partners, is a manifestation of this preference. However, contemporary egalitarian norms challenge these preferences, creating systemic friction.

The mismatch between evolved mate-selection strategies and modern social conditioning contributes to relationship dissatisfaction, mate-value distortion, and demographic pair-bonding decline. Game-theoretic models, such as the costly signaling model, suggest that women use signals like hypergamy to assess a man's quality and commitment. When cultural norms discourage traditional dominance-submission hierarchies, these signals become distorted, leading to miscommunication and relationship dissatisfaction. Quantitative analysis of demographic data reveals a decline in pair-bonding rates, with women increasingly delaying marriage or opting out of it altogether. This decline can be attributed to the structural mismatch between evolved preferences for high-status partners and modern social norms that emphasize equality and independence.

The systemic friction introduced by this mismatch can be measured by analyzing the correlation between relationship satisfaction and adherence to traditional gender roles. Studies have shown that couples who adhere to traditional gender roles tend to report higher relationship satisfaction than those who do not. This suggests that the tension between evolved mate-selection strategies and modern social norms is a significant contributor to relationship dissatisfaction. Furthermore, the distortion of mate-value signaling due to cultural norms can lead to misaligned expectations and decreased relationship quality. The quantifiable analysis of these dynamics can provide insights into the mechanisms driving the decline in pair-bonding rates and relationship dissatisfaction.
