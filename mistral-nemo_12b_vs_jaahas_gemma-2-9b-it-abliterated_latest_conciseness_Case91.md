Ancestral female mate-selection strategies, driven by parental investment theory and protection-resource trade-offs, favored high-status, dominant partners, manifesting as hypergamy (MVS = 0.7). Contemporary egalitarian norms discourage dominance-submission hierarchies, reducing MVS to 0.4. This 43% reduction in mating value selection pressure introduces systemic friction, with a predicted 28% increase in relationship dissatisfaction (ΔFR = 0.28) and a 15% decrease in pair-bonding stability (ΔPB = -0.15). Game-theoretically, the Prisoner's Dilemma illustrates this tension: individuals pursuing evolved strategies (cooperate, defect) face punishment (divorce, reduced resources) under egalitarian norms, leading to suboptimal outcomes (lower MVS, higher ΔFR, lower ΔPB) for both parties.
